## Mentoring

I have mentored four master's students at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT) and two undergraduate researchers at Princeton, and I have co-mentored doctoral researchers at both institutions. Their backgrounds span electrical engineering, computer science, mathematics, and mechatronics.

My mentoring emphasizes a progression from guided problem formulation to independent research. I ask students to connect mathematical structure with computation and physical interpretation, explain what they have learned and what remains uncertain, and propose the next step themselves. Two of my master's mentees continued to doctoral study at KIT and TU Darmstadt, and both became co-authors on peer-reviewed research with me.

Current and recent mentoring topics include:

- GPU-accelerated optimization for large-scale linear and mixed-integer models;
- sensitivity-based model reduction and acceleration for nonlinear power systems;
- maximum-loadability screening for data center interconnections; and
- sensitivity analysis for large-scale energy-system planning.

## Teaching

From 2022 to 2024, I served as a guest lecturer in *Energy Informatics II* at KIT, teaching nonlinear programming and optimization methods for power systems.

My teaching approach makes the reasoning behind difficult ideas visible. I use small physical examples to introduce mathematical formulations, then move toward computational projects in which students must justify assumptions, verify results, and communicate limitations. I am prepared to teach optimization, control, numerical methods, and power-system analysis, and I am interested in developing a project-based graduate elective titled *Computational Optimization for Networked Energy Systems*.
