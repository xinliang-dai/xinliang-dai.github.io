I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the [ZERO Lab](https://zero.lab.princeton.edu/) at Princeton University's Andlinger Center for Energy and the Environment, working with Prof. Jesse D. Jenkins. I develop computational methods that exploit problem structure for the real-time operation and long-term planning of large-scale networked energy infrastructure.

My research combines graph-based distributed optimization, sensitivity-based dimensional reduction and network analysis, and GPU-accelerated computation. I use these methods to develop scalable power flow and AC optimal power flow algorithms, coordinate transmission and distribution systems, and accelerate generation and transmission expansion planning. A central goal is to understand when reduced models, approximations, and accelerated solutions can be trusted.

I received my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering and Information Technology from the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT) in 2025, advised by Prof. Veit Hagenmeyer. I previously earned an M.Sc. from KIT and a B.Sc. from Jilin University, and studied as an exchange student at National Tsing-Hua University. My work has included research visits at Mines Paris-PSL and the University of Freiburg, as well as collaborations with TransnetBW and Hitachi Energy.
