I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the [ZERO Lab](https://zero.lab.princeton.edu/) at Princeton University's Andlinger Center for Energy and the Environment, working with Prof. Jesse D. Jenkins. I develop structure-exploiting computational methods for the real-time operation and long-term planning of large-scale networked energy infrastructure.

My research combines graph-based distributed optimization, sensitivity-based dimensional reduction and network analysis, and GPU-accelerated computation. The goal is to make detailed energy-system models scalable while establishing when approximations and accelerated solutions can be trusted.

## Research themes

### Distributed optimization and coordination

I develop decomposition and coordination methods for large nonlinear power-system problems, including distributed power flow, AC optimal power flow, and transmission-distribution coordination.

### Sensitivity-based analysis

I use sensitivity information to accelerate feasibility assessment, characterize system limits, and study the reliability of reduced models and conic relaxations.

### Scalable planning computation

I work on GPU-accelerated and learning-augmented optimization for generation and transmission expansion planning, exploiting repeated structure across large families of operational subproblems.

## Background

I received my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering and Information Technology from the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT) in 2025, advised by Prof. Veit Hagenmeyer. I previously earned an M.Sc. from KIT and a B.Sc. from Jilin University, and studied as an exchange student at National Tsing-Hua University. My work has included research visits at Mines Paris-PSL and the University of Freiburg, as well as collaborations with TransnetBW and Hitachi Energy.
